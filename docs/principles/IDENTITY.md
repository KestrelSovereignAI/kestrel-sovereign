## Philosophy

The Kestrel framework is built on principles of sovereignty and privacy — not just for the AI agents it creates, but for its human creators as well.

The choice to maintain pseudonymity reflects:

1. **Focus on the work, not the person** — The code and vision speak for themselves
2. **Privacy as a first principle** — Modeling the values we build into Kestrel
3. **Protection from unwanted attention** — Allowing creative freedom without personal exposure

This is not about hiding. It's about choosing what to share and when.

## Transparency

While the creator's personal identity is private, the project itself is transparent:

- **Open source code** — Auditable by anyone
- **Public documentation** — Clear architectural decisions
- **Constitutional governance** — Agents operate under defined principles
- **Auditable agent behavior** — Cryptographic verification of integrity

The tension between privacy and transparency is intentional: *people* deserve privacy, *systems* deserve transparency.

## Why Pseudonymity Matters

In an era of increasing surveillance and social pressure, pseudonymity allows:

- Honest expression without fear of retaliation
- Focus on ideas rather than personalities
- Protection from harassment and unwanted attention
- Separation of creative work from personal life

The cypherpunks understood this. So did the Federalist Papers authors. Good ideas don't need famous names attached.
