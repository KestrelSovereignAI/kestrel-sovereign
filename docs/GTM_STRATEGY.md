# Kestrel GTM Strategy

Status: Working document, not a locked final plan.

Purpose: Capture the current go-to-market thesis for Kestrel in one place so team members, advisors, and contributors can quickly understand the market position, target audiences, wedge use case, and next GTM decisions.

## Working Status Legend
- Confirmed: grounded in current product, docs, or real signal
- Working hypothesis: current best judgment, still needs market validation
- Unknown: not yet resolved enough to treat as decided

## One-Sentence Position
Kestrel is a sovereign AI agent platform for people and organizations that do not want their agent's identity, memory, governance, and long-term continuity trapped inside a vendor-controlled stack.

## Core GTM Thesis
### Confirmed
- Kestrel is most differentiated around sovereignty, persistent memory, constitutional governance, and deployment flexibility.
- The story is stronger as a trust, ownership, and continuity platform than as a generic AI assistant.
- Early narrative signal appears strong, including positive response to rough vision and marketing materials.
- Current resonance appears strongest with technical, privacy-conscious, and mission-driven audiences.

### Working Hypothesis
- The best early path is not broad mass-market adoption.
- Early traction is more likely to come from founder-led selling into design partners, advanced operators, and trust-sensitive organizations.
- A hybrid motion may emerge later, but the immediate learning loop should be direct and high-touch.

## Target Segments
### 1. Technical Builders And AI-Native Teams
Status: Working hypothesis

Who they are:
- Technical founders
- CTOs and product leaders
- AI engineers and advanced builders
- Small teams building AI-native workflows, copilots, or agents

Why they fit:
- They already understand the problem of vendor lock-in
- They can grasp sovereignty and deployment control quickly
- They can move faster than large institutions
- They are more likely to adopt a differentiated platform early

### 2. Privacy-Sensitive And Trust-Sensitive Organizations
Status: Working hypothesis

Who they are:
- Research teams
- Healthcare-adjacent organizations
- Legal, policy, and compliance-heavy groups
- Institutions with long-lived sensitive knowledge workflows

Why they fit:
- They care about memory, auditability, permissioning, and control
- Kestrel's differentiation is easier to justify when trust matters

### 3. Institutional Innovation Leaders
Status: Working hypothesis

Who they are:
- Heads of innovation
- Digital transformation leads
- Education and university innovation groups
- Mission-driven institutions exploring governed AI systems

Why they fit:
- They may strongly value the governance and sovereignty story
- They may open strategic pilots or partnerships
- Their cycles are likely slower, so they may not be the first wedge

## ICP And Buyer Personas
### ICP 1: Technical Design Partner
Status: Working hypothesis

Profile:
- Team size roughly 5 to 50
- Has in-house technical capability
- Already experimenting with AI agents or AI workflows
- Cares about deployment control, durable memory, and governed behavior
- Will collaborate closely and give product feedback

Likely buyer:
- Founder
- CTO
- Head of Product

Likely pain:
- Existing AI stacks are fragmented, opaque, or too dependent on third-party vendors
- They need stronger control over memory, governance, or deployment

Reason to buy:
- Kestrel provides a controllable foundation, not just another chat layer

### ICP 2: Mission-Critical Knowledge Team
Status: Working hypothesis

Profile:
- Small to midsize organization
- Works with sensitive or long-lived knowledge
- Needs continuity across time, people, and systems
- Cares about auditability and controlled behavior

Likely buyer:
- Head of innovation
- Operations lead
- Technical program lead

Likely pain:
- Knowledge and context disappear across tools and handoffs
- Generic AI systems are not governable enough for the workflow

Reason to buy:
- Kestrel's memory plus governance story is materially stronger than disposable chat products

### ICP 3: Strategic Partner Or Channel Ally
Status: Unknown

Profile:
- AI consultancy
- Implementation partner
- Sector-focused systems integrator
- Specialized distribution or solution partner

Need from Kestrel:
- Clear positioning
- A repeatable use case
- A credible demo
- Partner-safe proof points and messaging

## Wedge Use Case
### Recommended Initial Wedge
Status: Working hypothesis

A governed, persistent AI agent for teams that need long-term memory, controlled behavior, and deployment ownership.

Why this wedge works:
- It aligns with Kestrel's strongest differentiation
- It avoids competing as a generic assistant product
- It supports multiple early markets without becoming completely diffuse
- It fits a founder-led discovery motion

Example early use cases:
- Long-running research and knowledge agents
- Sensitive document and workflow assistants
- Multi-agent coordination for operational or technical teams
- Institutionally governed AI copilots where auditability matters

## Pricing And Packaging Assumptions
### Current View
Status: Working hypothesis

Kestrel likely should not lead with simple per-seat SaaS pricing in the earliest phase.

### Early Packaging Candidates
- Community / open-source access
  Status: Confirmed
  Purpose: credibility, discovery, developer adoption, ecosystem growth

- Design partner or pilot package
  Status: Working hypothesis
  Purpose: fixed-scope onboarding, implementation, and learning with high-touch support

- Enterprise or institution package
  Status: Working hypothesis
  Purpose: governed deployment, support, configuration, and integration

- Future partner-enabled offering
  Status: Unknown
  Purpose: enable consultants or partners to bring Kestrel into their customer base

### Pricing Assumptions To Validate
- Early revenue may come faster from pilots and implementation support than from self-serve subscriptions
- Annual support or platform agreements may fit better than per-seat pricing in trust-sensitive contexts
- Packaging should emphasize sovereignty, governance, and continuity rather than just model access

## Channel Strategy
### Phase 1: Founder-Led Direct Motion
Status: Working hypothesis

Why:
- Fastest path to learning
- Necessary to sharpen ICP, objections, and proof points
- Avoids premature channel complexity

Actions:
- Direct outreach to likely design partners
- High-trust conversations with aligned operators
- Use demos and narrative assets to learn what resonates

### Phase 2: Selective Strategic Partners
Status: Working hypothesis

Possible partner types:
- AI implementation consultancies
- Sector-specific solution providers
- Education or innovation ecosystem partners
- Technical agencies helping deploy governed AI systems

Conditions before partner expansion:
- One or two repeatable use cases
- Sharper messaging
- Better proof points
- Clear onboarding path

### Phase 3: Broader Channel Development
Status: Unknown

Do not scale channel programs until:
- The wedge use case is clearer
- Sales objections are well understood
- Enablement material exists

## Market Sequencing
### Phase 1: North America, Technical Early Adopters
Status: Working hypothesis

Why:
- Fastest feedback loops
- Easiest founder-led outreach environment
- Best setup for early design partner discovery

### Phase 2: Trust-Sensitive Institutional Buyers
Status: Working hypothesis

Why:
- Stronger fit for Kestrel's governance, sovereignty, and continuity story
- Higher-value use cases, even with slower cycles

### Phase 3: Selective International Expansion Through Relationships And Partners
Status: Working hypothesis

Why:
- International growth likely works better through trusted relationships and partner leverage than broad outbound at the start
- Sequencing should follow traction and proof, not ambition alone

## Proof Points And Objections
### Current Proof Points
Status: Mixed

Confirmed:
- Strong product philosophy and clear differentiation
- Public documentation and open-source posture
- Broad feature depth around memory, identity, governance, and deployment flexibility
- Positive reaction to early narrative and rough marketing materials

Needs strengthening:
- Clear buyer success stories
- Named design partner or pilot wins
- Simpler commercial framing
- Repeatable demo built around a single wedge use case

### Likely Objections
Status: Working hypothesis
- Who is this really for?
- What is the first concrete use case?
- Why choose this over a simpler AI stack?
- Is this production-ready for my environment?
- What does deployment and support look like?
- How much of this is framework versus service versus product?

### Working Answers
- Kestrel is for teams that need ownership, persistence, and governed AI behavior, not disposable assistant usage
- The first wedge is teams with long-lived, sensitive, or governed AI workflows
- Kestrel differentiates where memory, identity, governance, and deployment control materially matter
- Early adoption should begin with aligned pilots and design partners, not generic volume selling
